Make sense of your pet's bloodwork.

Drop your IDEXX VetConnect PLUS PDF reports onto the page and PetHUD turns them into clear trends over time, plain-language explanations of every value, condition screening, and a head start on your next vet visit — all in your browser. Trends at a glance

Every value, every visit — in one dense view

Each analyte becomes a compact sparkline so you can see the direction of travel at a glance. Out-of-range results are flagged and color-coded, and subtle markers show when a test wasn't run or when something in your pet's care changed.

What's an "analyte"? It's the lab's word for a single thing measured in a blood or urine sample — creatinine, glucose, a white-blood-cell count. One report holds dozens; PetHUD gives each its own card and its own history so you can follow it over time.
Honest charts

Graphs that show what's missing, not just what's there

A flat line can fool you. If a test wasn't repeated at the latest visit, most tools just keep drawing the last value — so a number that's months stale looks reassuringly steady. PetHUD instead draws a dashed marker exactly where a result is absent, so you can tell "unchanged" apart from "not measured this time" and never misread a gap as good news. Care events you log appear on the same timeline, so cause and effect line up.

Cited context

Understand what each value means — sourced, not invented

Open any result for a plain-language explanation: what the test measures, what a high or low value may suggest, what moves it, how quickly to expect change, and the related metrics to read alongside it. Every statement links to respected references — Cornell eClinPath, the IRIS kidney-staging guidelines, ACVIM and AAFP consensus statements. PetHUD deliberately points you to those sources and to your vet rather than handing you a verdict of its own.

Condition screening

Disease-oriented panels & IRIS staging

Panels for the conditions that matter in older pets — CKD, hyperthyroidism, heart disease (HCM), IBD / small-cell lymphoma, diabetes and more — gather the relevant markers in one place. For chronic kidney disease, your cat's IRIS stage is computed from its own creatinine and SDMA. These are screening prompts to raise with your vet, never diagnoses.

Age-aware vigilance

What's worth watching at your pet's age

A life-stage banner surfaces the conditions to keep an eye on for your pet's current age, split into what bloodwork can screen for versus what to watch clinically — with the guidelines it's drawn from.

Plain-language summaries

What each visit actually means

Every report opens with a readable synthesis: how many values are out of range, what changed since the last visit, and which multi-marker patterns are worth raising — each linking through to the relevant condition panel.

Visit prep

Walk in ready for your vet

PetHUD turns the latest results into a short prep list: how long it's been since the last panel and the typical recheck cadence, plus specific questions to consider asking — all grounded in your own pet's results.

Your own log

Add the context only you have

Bloodwork makes more sense alongside weight and life events. Record weight and note medications, diet changes or symptoms — weight gets its own trend, and events appear as markers on every chart, so cause and effect are easy to see.

A companion, not a replacement

PetHUD helps you ask better questions — it doesn't answer them for you

Every explanation here is drawn from published veterinary references and points back to them, and to your veterinary team. PetHUD will not diagnose your pet, will not tell you what to do, and is not a substitute for a professional exam. What it does is help you walk into the room understanding the trends, knowing which results to ask about, and able to follow the answers.

Why PetHUD exists

Our pets can't tell us how they feel, and they age faster than we're ready for. Bloodwork is one of the few honest windows we get — but it arrives as a dense PDF of numbers and reference ranges, usually at a stressful moment, and it's genuinely hard to know which numbers matter, how a value compares to last year, or how urgent any of it really is.

Even with a caring vet, the questions only occur to you later. It's hard to know what to ask in the room, hard to hold a year of results in your head, and life gets in the way — work, distance, money, the sheer hope that our hearty companion is fine — of tending to the creatures we love most. A trend quietly drifting in the wrong direction can hide in plain sight.

PetHUD was built to close that gap a little: to turn scattered reports into a clear story over time, to explain the jargon in plain language with real sources behind it, and to hand you a short list of things worth raising — so that the conversation with your vet starts further along, and nothing important slips past simply because it was hard to see.

Private by design

Your reports are parsed and stored entirely in your browser using IndexedDB — nothing is ever uploaded to a server. Bring your own PDFs; clear them any time.

In memoriam

For P'tit Loup

I built PetHUD after my dear cat P'tit Loup died suddenly in May 2026 from acute kidney injury and related conditions, after fourteen beautiful years. My other cat, Iris — a female of the same age — had already been treated for early-stage chronic kidney disease. But P'tit Loup was such a hearty goofball that, aside from some slightly elevated BUN and a temporary dip in weight about half a year earlier, he showed almost no sign of ailing health until his very last weeks.

I keep wondering whether I'd have seen it sooner if those scattered numbers had ever lined up into a picture I could read. This project is my attempt to give other people that picture.

Dedicated to the memory of P'tit Loup, and to his sometimes reluctantly loving sister Iris — whom I'm now caring for very closely.

PetHUD exists thanks to the knowledge I gained from the wonderful vet techs, doctors and staff at Fremont Animal Hospital, the Veterinary Emergency Group, and the Animal Medical Center of Seattle. Through harrowing circumstances they were endlessly supportive and patient, and they taught me how to care for P'tit Loup and Iris. Without them this project would not exist. I hope it helps other cat and dog lovers build the same kind of trust with their own veterinary teams.

PetHUD is an educational tool to help you read and discuss your pet's bloodwork. It is not a diagnosis and does not replace veterinary care.
